# Individual Reflection: guide

The brief asks each member for a **one-page reflection linking the project to at least two units**. This is your own work, so this file does not write it for you. It only helps you plan it.

Write it in your own words, from what **you** did. Examiners can ask you about it.

## What to include

1. **Your role(s).** Which of the four roles did you hold, and in which weeks?
2. **What you did.** Name your real commits, pull requests, tests or documents (link to them).
3. **Two or more units.** For each unit, say which idea from that unit you used in FindIt and where it shows. Be specific: a file, a diagram, a decision, a test.
4. **What went wrong and what you learned.** One honest example.
5. **What you would do differently.**
6. **Use of AI tools.** If you used an AI assistant for code, documents or explanations, say what for and what you checked yourself. Follow your course's rules on this.

## Prompts to link units to the project

These are examples. Use the unit names from your own course outline.

- **Requirements and analysis:** how the SRS requirements (FR and NFR numbers) came from the problem, and one requirement you changed.
- **Modelling and design:** which UML diagram you drew or checked, and what it showed you.
- **Databases:** why the tables are split the way they are (for example, why a found item's private detail is in its own table).
- **Security:** a threat from the design document and how the code defends against it.
- **Testing:** one test you wrote or ran, the rule it protects, and a bug that a test caught.
- **Project management:** a risk from the register, how the sprint plan changed, and what rotating roles was like.
- **Web development and APIs:** one route you followed from the page to the database and back.

## Format

One page: about 350 to 450 words, your name, student number and role history at the top.
